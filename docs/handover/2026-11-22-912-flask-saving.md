## 2026-11-22: Flask saving, melee is infinite, flasks are not (#912)

**Done.** Three new settings in `borg-core/src/borg_fight_attack.rs`, all compiled and shipped off (0 / defaults), so play is unchanged (smoke 3/3 identical):

- `attack_flask_saving` (mode 0-2). In the ordinary attack chooser (the throw branch of `borg_launch_damage_one`), a flask of oil is rated 0 against a monster while melee is enough. Mode 1 covers an adjacent monster; mode 2 also covers one coming to melee anyway (awake, able to move, no ranged attack; `flask_saving_reaches`). "Enough" (`melee_is_enough`) means:
  - melee is open: not afraid, not confused;
  - it kills within `attack_flask_melee_rounds` (3) player turns at `town_melee_damage`'s real damage (blows, hit chance, the HP cap);
  - the danger over the turns melee needs beyond the flask's stays below `attack_flask_danger_percent` (50) of the HP left.
- Attack on sight and the focused planner keep the oil (the grey mushroom patch, #869).

**Found.**
- **A first version counted every melee turn's danger and never fired.** The Borg's danger for a white worm mass is 30 against a CL2 Warrior's 41 HP, although melee (14.5 HP a turn) kills the 10-HP worm in one turn, as the flask does. Only melee's extra turns are extra risk.
- **Most flasks are thrown at range:** in the named Warrior game, 83 of 88. Mode 1 alone changes little, which is why mode 2 exists.
- **Named games**, 100,000 turns, local and directional: mode 2 cuts the flasks thrown in every class (Warrior 88→57, Paladin 105→43, Rogue 84→49, Priest 35→16, Necromancer 47→38, Mage 40→29). Every game lives to the cap. The Warrior and Paladin go deeper with more experience. The table is in `docs/CALIBRATION.md`.
- **The overrides.** With mode 2, the melee classes' point-blank overrides (#901) are redundant in five of the six games. The Rogue, whose arrow divisor is 3, plays differently without its override.
- In the smoke Warrior (seed 101), mode 1 without the Warrior override reproduces the shipped game key for key: the rule makes the same choice the override did.

**Checked.** CI's Rust checks (borg-core 654 tests, 4 new `flask_saving_tests` on the pure rule); `check-param-citations.py`; `scripts/tests`; `borg-core-vendor.py check`; smoke 3/3 identical; `engine/tests/test_stair_handoff.py` OK.

**Not checked / left.**
- `flask_saving_reaches` and the live gate read engine state and have no unit test; the named games exercise them.
- At range, the saving trusts that the Borg then waits or closes in; whether that costs HP against faster monsters is for the zoo.
- **For the owner:**
  - Whether to ship mode 2 and remove the per-class point-blank overrides (#901).
  - The two thresholds (3 turns, 50%) are hypotheses.
  - A borg-zoo arm should measure it first (RULES 14, 20): shipped vs `attack_flask_saving` 2, and 2 without the overrides, per class, with the flasks thrown, the flasks left at each town return and the deaths.

Two things remain unknown: whether flasks are actually saved at scale (not counted), and whether the 0.15 lower CL is real. A count of flasks thrown and carried per class from the retained evidence, or a rerun on this merged branch, would settle the first. `save2-noov` is no better than `save2` (9 deaths against 7), so the #901 overrides stay.

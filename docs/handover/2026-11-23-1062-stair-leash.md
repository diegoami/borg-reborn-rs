## 2026-11-23: The holes in C's stair leash (#1062)

Every check below ran on the **default core, borg-rs** (`borg-core/`), built
from `origin/track-p` `f67fad19` plus this branch. The reborn core is not
touched.

### What was done

A new module, `borg-core/src/borg_leash.rs` (ours, listed in
`scripts/borg-core-vendor.py`'s OURS), and five parameters read in
`leash_holes` (`borg_flow.rs`, the registry's `flow_` file). Every switch is
compiled and shipped **0**, which is C's play.

- **`flow_leash_any_stair`** (holes 1 and 3). The exploration leash goes
  through `borg_leash::beyond`, which with the switches off is exactly C's
  `borg_flow_cost_stair(..) > limit`. It is used by
  `borg_flow_far_from_stairs_dist` (the dark, kill and take flows),
  `take_select`'s own stair check (`borg_flow_take.rs`) and the vein flow
  (`borg_flow_misc.rs`). With the switch on, one flow spreads from every
  known staircase of either kind, so the nearest one counts, by flow steps.
  An unreachable staircase no longer stands for all of them, and C bug 31
  (`borg_flow_dark` keeps the farthest up staircase) goes too. The return
  leash in `borg_think_dungeon` measures the distance to the nearest
  staircase of either kind. When it fires it sets `leash.return_any`, and
  the two `goal.less` walks then try `borg_flow_stair_both` before the up
  staircases. `return_any` is cleared with `goal.less` ("Close enough", or
  any other clear seen at the next leash check) and on a new level, so the
  other `goal.less` users ("Needing to get back on correct depth", the
  scumming climb) still go up.
- **`flow_leash_unknown_stair_steps`** = N (hole 2). With no staircase the
  exploration leash counts, or none reachable, the leash is N flow steps
  from the grid where the Borg arrived on the level. That grid is recorded
  at its first update there (`borg_leash::update`, called from
  `borg_update` after the map). The return leash is unchanged here: there
  is no goal that walks back to the arrival grid.
- **`flow_leash_teleported_recall`**, with `flow_leash_recall_max_level` (5)
  and `flow_leash_recall_steps` (30). The update notes a jump of more than
  one grid between two updates. The next dungeon think measures the flow
  steps from every known staircase, in an observation that puts the flow
  state back, so `borg_flow_old` is untouched. If that is more than the
  steps, or no staircase is known or reachable, Word of Recall is read at
  the first think at danger 0. The check sits after `fight_before_flight`
  and the lightless recovery, before "Needing to get back on correct
  depth". It is judged once per jump and forgotten on a new level, when
  Recall is already active, or when none is carried.

**Town is always C's.** The first replays had `flow_leash_any_stair` on in
town, where the down staircase started a return leash every few steps: two
Borgs never left town in 100,000 turns. C's leashes never act in town
because town has no up staircase, so all three switches now act only in the
dungeon.

### The review of #1087 (Codex, GPT-6, posted by the owner)

It reviewed `476aabb7` and raised four P2 findings, all fixed on top of the
merge with `track-p` `bf8d2604`:

1. **The return leash measured grids.** A staircase two grids away across a
   wall hid a 30-step walk. With `flow_leash_any_stair` a return now starts
   on walking steps (`return_leash`).
2. **A nearby down staircase ended any `goal.less`.** That included the
   depth correction's climb to an up staircase. Only a return this leash
   started (`return_any`) now ends on the either-kind steps. Every other
   `goal.less` ends on C's distance to an up staircase (`return_distances`).
3. **Danger at the arrival grid lifted the arrival leash.**
   `borg_flow_enqueue_grid` refuses a dangerous seed. Every distance a
   switch measures now comes from `steps_map`: walking steps over the known
   map, with the danger check off (`flight_no_danger`, earlier icky marks
   cleared). The flow state is put back afterwards. It is spread once a
   think for each set of sources (staircases, arrival grid) and cached in
   `LeashState::maps`, instead of once per candidate grid. A grid the
   arrival grid does not reach is not judged far.
4. **A Phase Door spent a Recall.** A new parameter,
   `flow_leash_recall_min_jump` (12 grids), counts only a teleport:
   - Phase Door is `TELEPORT:10` (object.txt), randomised by a quarter in
     `effect_handler_TELEPORT` (effect-handler-general.c), so it lands 9 to
     11 grids away.
   - The teleport trap and the Teleportation scroll are `TELEPORT:M80` and
     `:M60`.
   - 2 restores "any jump".

### The re-review of #1087 (Codex, GPT-6, posted by the owner)

It reviewed `5e7d827a` and confirmed that the down-stair check now keeps
unrelated up-stair goals. It raised three P2 findings, all fixed:

1. **Unknown grids were crossed.** The danger-free flow (`avoid = false`)
   crossed unknown grids, so an unexplored gap looked like a shortcut.
   `steps_map` is now a pure breadth-first search, `terrain_steps`, over
   known terrain. It uses the flow's own wall rules (`walkable`: no wall,
   rubble but passable rubble, unknown grid, or lava without fire
   immunity). The dark flow aims at unknown grids, and such a target is one
   step past its nearest reached neighbour (`steps_at`, the frontier).
2. **A monster in the corridor lifted the arrival leash.** The search
   ignores monsters, traps and danger: they are what the Borg meets on the
   way, not the way. It no longer touches the flow state at all
   (`with_flow_saved` is gone).
3. **Phase Door told by its source.** `JumpCause` marks the next jump from
   the success point of each action (after its keys):
   - `borg_read_scroll`, `borg_use_staff[_fail]`, `borg_activate_item` and
     `borg_spell`;
   - the message "You hit a teleport trap!" (`borg_messages.rs`).

   A trap or the Borg's own teleport (Teleportation scroll, staff,
   activation, Teleport Self, Portal, Shadow Shift) always counts. Its own
   Phase Door or Dimension Door never does. Only a jump of unknown cause (a
   monster's teleport-to) is judged by `flow_leash_recall_min_jump`.
